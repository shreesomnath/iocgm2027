**Title:** Beyond the Snapshot: A Dynamic Multi-Sensor GeoAI Framework for Real-Time Landslide Susceptibility Evolution and Cross-Basin Transferability in Nepal’s Mid-Hills

**Introduction:** The Himalayan region is acutely vulnerable to monsoon-induced landslides. Traditional Landslide Susceptibility Mapping (LSM) often relies on static topographic factors, failing to capture near-real-time environmental dynamics. Recent literature highlights a critical gap in operationalizing dynamic, Explainable AI (XAI) models that are generalizable across varying topographies. This study proposes an open-data GeoAI framework utilizing multi-sensor fusion to model the temporal evolution of landslide susceptibility (2021–2026) in Nepal, directly addressing national risk management priorities.

**Methods:** The methodology integrates time-series Sentinel-1 SAR (moisture/roughness proxies) and Sentinel-2 optical data (vegetation indices) with ALOS PALSAR DEM derivatives via Google Earth Engine (GEE). An ablation study is designed to quantify the value of sensor synergy versus static topography. An Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) model is trained using historical landslide inventories (NASA GLC and Durham/BGS) in the Arun River Basin. To ensure operational robustness, the framework undergoes a rigorous cross-basin spatial transferability test in the distinct Trishuli Basin. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is employed to maintain physical consistency and interpret feature influence.

**Results:** Preliminary workflows indicate that the synergistic Model C (Topography + S1 + S2) significantly outperforms static baselines, particularly in reducing false negatives during peak monsoon periods. The XGBoost architecture demonstrates high computational efficiency, while SHAP analysis reveals that temporal SAR backscatter variations are dominant predictors preceding failure events. The cross-basin transferability test yields robust AUC-ROC and F1-scores, validating the framework's geographic generalization capabilities.

**Conclusions:** Integrating multi-sensor Earth Observation data within an Explainable GeoAI framework provides a dynamic, scalable solution for landslide risk management. By proving cross-basin transferability using entirely open-access data, this study offers a deployable tool for local governments in data-scarce mountainous terrains.
